Background: where this project sits in the grant framework

California is developing a public wildfire catastrophe model under SB 429. The California Department of Insurance (CDI) describes the overall system in the Public Wildfire Catastrophe Model Fact Sheet. Technical expectations for insurers and modelers appear in CDI’s Wildfire Catastrophe Model Checklist (PRID, updated February 2025).

A full catastrophe model is a pipeline, not one regression. At a high level:

Module Question it answers Examples in production models
Exposures What is at risk, where, and under what policy terms? Geocoded homes, replacement cost, limits, deductibles
Hazard / event generation How often and how intensely can fire reach a location? Fuels, weather, fire simulators, burn probability, stochastic event catalogs
Vulnerability Given fire exposure, how much physical damage? Damage vs flame length / embers given construction, defensible space
Financial / actuarial Given damage, what insured loss? Policy rules, aggregation, loss costs for rate filings

\[\text{Exposures} \;\rightarrow\; p(\mathcal{F}\mid x) \;\rightarrow\; p(d \mid s,\,\mathcal{F}) \;\rightarrow\; \mathbb{E}[\text{loss} \mid d,\,\pi]\]

where \(x\) is location/value, \(s\) is structure characteristics, \(\mathcal{F}\) is fire exposure in a specific event, \(d\) is damage, and \(\pi\) is policy terms.

Weather and climate enter in the hazard module (they drive simulators and event catalogs). They are not usually fed directly into the vulnerability model.

This stepping stone is a small slice of the vulnerability module:

We are estimating damage severity given structures already inspected in or near a wildfire.

# Background: where this project sits in the grant framework

California is developing a **public wildfire catastrophe model** under [SB 429](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202320240SB429). The California Department of Insurance (CDI) describes the overall system in the [Public Wildfire Catastrophe Model Fact Sheet](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/01-consumers/180-climate-change/upload/FOR-PUBLICATION-WILDFIRE-MODEL-FACT-SHEET-3.pdf). Technical expectations for insurers and modelers appear in CDI’s [Wildfire Catastrophe Model Checklist](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/01-consumers/180-climate-change/upload/WildfireCatastropheModelChecklist_PRID_20250219.pdf) (PRID, updated February 2025).

A full catastrophe model is a **pipeline**, not one regression. At a high level:

| Module | Question it answers | Examples in production models |
|--------|---------------------|------------------------------|
| **Exposures** | What is at risk, where, and under what policy terms? | Geocoded homes, replacement cost, limits, deductibles |
| **Hazard / event generation** | How often and how intensely can fire reach a location? | Fuels, weather, fire simulators, burn probability, stochastic event catalogs |
| **Vulnerability** | Given fire exposure, how much physical damage? | Damage vs flame length / embers given construction, defensible space |
| **Financial / actuarial** | Given damage, what insured loss? | Policy rules, aggregation, loss costs for rate filings |

$$\text{Exposures} \;\rightarrow\; p(\mathcal{F}\mid x) \;\rightarrow\; p(d \mid s,\,\mathcal{F}) \;\rightarrow\; \mathbb{E}[\text{loss} \mid d,\,\pi]$$

where $x$ is location/value, $s$ is structure characteristics, $\mathcal{F}$ is fire exposure in a specific event, $d$ is damage, and $\pi$ is policy terms.

Weather and climate enter in the **hazard** module (they drive simulators and event catalogs). They are **not** usually fed directly into the vulnerability model.

This stepping stone is a **small slice** of the **vulnerability** module:
